# Corpus Analysis for Chunking Strategy — docs/

Analysis-only report. No files in `docs/` were modified. Generated by
reading every `.txt` file's raw stats (length, line structure, table-row
density) plus manual inspection of 2-4 representative files per folder.

`handbook/` contains a single 11MB PDF (`AUPP-Student-Handbook-2026.pdf`),
**no `.txt` files exist there** — see Anomalies and Recommendations.

## Per-Folder Detail

### programs/ (55 files)
- **Length**: min 256, median 3,485, p90 6,759, max 10,026 chars. Only 3
  files (5.5%) fit in an 800-char chunk whole; 8 (14.5%) fit in 1,500.
  Whole-file chunking is not viable here — these are long pages.
- **Lines**: median line 65 chars, p95 243, longest single line 922 chars
  (`english-preparatory-program.txt`, a dense prose paragraph). Blank lines
  are only 2.4% of all lines — paragraph breaks are sparse, so
  paragraph-based splitting alone won't produce many natural cut points.
- **Tables**: 938 table-row lines, 36.3% of all lines — the highest table
  density in the corpus by a wide margin. **20 files have a table-row run
  (consecutive table lines) exceeding 800 chars**, and 2 files
  (`bachelor-of-arts-in-architecture.txt`,
  `bachelor-of-science-in-civil-engineering.txt`) exceed 1,500 chars in a
  single run. A naive `CharacterTextSplitter` on `\n` with a hard
  `chunk_size` **will** cut through the middle of a course-table run in
  these files, because the run alone is longer than the chunk. Table-heaviest
  files: the two IR&D dual/single-degree pages (67 and 64 table lines out
  of ~80 total) and the tourism/hospitality BBA page (60/85).
- **Shape**: every program page I read (civil engineering, business admin
  variants) follows the same skeleton: `Program Description` prose →
  `Career Opportunities` prose → a `Program Structure: ... | Required Total
  Credits (...): N` mini-table → blank → `Course Code: X | Course Name: Y |
  Credit: Z` rows grouped under a subheading (General Education, Major
  Courses, Electives) → a closing `Note:` block. Prose and tables are two
  genuinely different content types living in the same file — see
  Recommendations for why I think they still don't need *different global
  settings*, just a splitter that respects table-row boundaries.
- **Cross-references**: mild — "Click here for more information about the
  General Education courses" and "The list of technical electives varies
  each year. The list is available from the VPASA office." point outside
  the file. Not rule/exception pairs, low risk if split.
- **Anomalies**: `certificate-courses.txt` (202 body chars) is a bare link
  list/hub page, not real content — see §5. Six
  `certificate-courses-*.txt` files use a `Label:\nvalue` two-line
  convention (consistent with faculty pages) rather than the pipe-table
  convention; not broken, just a second legitimate label:value pattern to
  be aware of.

### scholarships/ (11 files)
- **Length**: min 613, median 1,640, p90 2,014, max 2,038. Much tighter
  spread than programs — these are short pages. Only 1 file fits in 800
  chars; 3 (27%) fit in 1,500.
- **Lines**: median 36 chars, p95 174, max line 530 chars. Blank lines
  3.5%.
- **Tables**: only 2.6% table rows (8 lines total across the whole
  folder), concentrated in 2 files (the Academic Excellence and Financial
  Need scholarship pages, which end in a percentage/duration table like
  `Number of (A): 7 | Dual Degree: 35% | Duration: 2 Years | Single
  Degree: 35% | Duration: 4 Years`). No table run exceeds 800 chars here —
  tables are short (5-7 rows).
- **Shape**: every file I read follows `Application Period` →
  `Criteria and Eligibility` prose → `Required Document` list →
  `Major Offered` → (sometimes) a short percentage table. Data-dense but
  genuinely short; a scholarship's eligibility criteria and its document
  requirements are usually only 1-2 short paragraphs apart, so keeping
  a whole file (or close to it) together preserves the file's inherent
  cause/effect logic (criteria → what documents you need to prove it).
- **Cross-references**: none observed.
- **Anomalies**: none.

### faculty/ (53 files)
- **Length**: min 620, median 1,408, p90 2,594, max 4,137. **This
  contradicts the "short atomic profile" assumption on its own** — median
  is close to leadership's, but the tail is long: 40% of files (21 of 53)
  do NOT fit in a 1,500-char chunk.
- **Lines**: median line only 17 chars (short label lines like
  `Position:`, `Department:`) but p95 jumps to 543 and the max single line
  is 1,550 chars (`faculty-yusuf-nulla.txt`) — one whole bio paragraph
  with no internal line breaks. Blank lines are 7.0%, the highest of any
  non-trivial folder, because these pages alternate short label lines and
  paragraph lines.
- **Tables**: none (0.0%).
- **Shape**: every profile follows `Name` → `Position:` / value →
  `Department:` / value → `E-mail:` / value → `Biography` → 1-4 prose
  paragraphs, each written as a single un-wrapped line. Short bios
  (Chandara Koem, 2 short paragraphs) fit easily in 1,500 chars; long
  bios (Yusuf Nulla, 6 paragraphs) don't. Because paragraphs are already
  discrete lines, a paragraph-boundary-aware splitter only has to make
  hard cuts on the ~21 files whose combined bio exceeds the cap — and even
  then it cuts between paragraphs, not mid-sentence, except for the rare
  single paragraph like Nulla's that's 1,550 chars on its own.
- **Anomalies**: none structural. Shortest file is 555 body chars
  (Kenneth Sutherland) — thin but legitimate (short real bio, not a
  scrape failure).

### academic-policies/ (39 files) — bimodal, most important folder to get right
- **Length**: min 174, median 873 (looks short!), but **p90 is 11,436 and
  max is 161,455** — the single biggest outlier in the entire corpus
  (`course-descriptions.txt`, 162KB). This folder is NOT uniformly "dense
  prose, needs bigger chunks" — it's two very different populations: ~25
  short single-topic policy stubs (150-900 chars) and a handful of massive
  reference documents.
- **Lines**: median 24, p95 471, max single line 2,316 chars
  (`course-descriptions.txt` — one course's full paragraph description).
  Blank lines only 1.6%, the lowest of any folder — very few natural
  paragraph breaks to split on.
- **Tables**: 405 table-row lines, 10.6% of all lines, almost entirely
  concentrated in `academic-calendar.txt` (254 of 306 lines — 83% table
  rows, format `Semester: X | Week: Y | Event: Z | Date: W`) and
  `course-descriptions.txt` (151 of 819 lines). **`academic-calendar.txt`
  is the one file in the whole corpus whose table-row run exceeds both the
  800-char AND 1,500-char thresholds** — its calendar-event rows run for
  thousands of characters uninterrupted, so any splitter must be able to
  break between individual `Semester: ... | Date: ...` rows without
  corrupting one.
- **Shape**: `course-descriptions.txt` is structurally NOT one document —
  it's ~261 independent course records (`CODE - Name` / `Credit: N | Pre-
  Requisite: ...` / one prose paragraph), concatenated. It should be
  treated as a mini-database, not prose. `academic-and-student-policies-
  and-regulations.txt` (55,611 chars, 588 lines) genuinely is dense prose —
  a concatenation of ~20 distinct sub-policies (Academic Advising, Academic
  Honors, Academic Integrity, ...) each a few paragraphs, one after another
  with no markdown-style headers, just a bare title line.
- **Cross-references**: `academic-and-student-policies-and-regulations.txt`
  line 508 and `graduation-application-and-requirements.txt` line 9 both
  contain "...unless otherwise provided for in a Dual Degree program..." —
  a rule with an inline exception in the same sentence, so it survives
  chunking fine as long as sentences aren't split mid-way. The bigger risk
  in this file is a topic's rule and its consequence landing in different
  chunks purely by char-count bad luck (e.g., "Summa Cum Laude" GPA bands
  are three tightly-linked one-line facts under `Graduation Distinction` —
  don't want a hard 800-char boundary landing between them).
- **Anomalies**: `academic-information.txt` (119 body chars) and several
  other sub-200-char files (`course-add-drop.txt` 215) are legitimate but
  extremely thin — see §5. `academic-calendar.txt` also contains one
  `--- Linked document: <pdf-url> ---` marker (the only occurrence in the
  whole corpus) showing the scraper pulled in text from a linked PDF
  calendar — worth knowing this pattern exists if it recurs elsewhere.

### tuition/ (6 files)
- **Length**: min 523, median 804, p90 3,162, max 3,622. Half the files
  fit whole in an 800-char chunk.
- **Lines**: median 13 (very short lines), p95 186, max 733.
- **Tables**: table-row regex hit 0% — but that's because this folder's
  tables **were not converted to the `Header: value | ...` convention**.
- **Shape**: 4 of 6 files are short payment-instructions pages (474-737

  2 (`tuition-fees.txt`, `other-fees.txt`) contain fee-schedule tables.
- **Anomalies — real broken table found**: `tuition-fees.txt` has two
  Year/Fall-Semester/Spring-Semester tuition grids where each cell was
  scraped onto its own bare line with no header, e.g.:
  ```
  Year
  Fall Semester
  Spring Semester
  1
  $3,000
  $3,000
  2
  $3,000
  $3,000
  ```
  This appears twice in the file (Single Degree grid, Dual Degree grid —
  both use identical `$3,000`/`$4,500`-style repeating values), plus a
  third small grid for the English Preparatory Program. Verified with
  `grep -cE '^\$[0-9,]+$'`: 19 bare dollar-only lines in `tuition-fees.txt`

  table in the corpus, there is no way to recover which row/column a given
  `$3,000` belongs to from the line itself** — a chunk boundary (or even
  just semantic search) landing on an isolated `$3,000` line is
  contextless and will produce wrong answers to "how much is tuition in
  year 2?". This is exactly the `rowspan`-style silent-data-loss bug
  CLAUDE.md says the table-fix pass was built to catch — it just didn't
  reach these two files.

## Anomalies (corpus-wide, §5 of the task)

1. **Thin/stub files that are NOT failed scrapes but genuine link-list hub
   pages** (menu pages with almost no prose, just child-page titles):
   `programs/certificate-courses.txt` (202), `academic-policies/academic-
   information.txt` (119), `student-life/commencement.txt` (151),
   `library/library-library-staff.txt` (75, the shortest file in the
   corpus), `admissions/future-students-bachelors.txt` (318). These
   scraped correctly — the source pages genuinely have almost no body
   content, they're navigation hubs. Recommend excluding sub-~150-char
   files from the index entirely (they add retrieval noise without
   answering any real question) or merging them into their parent
   topic page rather than chunking them as standalone documents.

2. **Broken/unfixed flattened tables**: `tuition/tuition-fees.txt` and
   `tuition/other-fees.txt` (parking fee grid) — the only files in the
   corpus where a genuine multi-column table was scraped as bare
   one-value-per-line text with no `Header: value` labels at all. This is
   the exact `rowspan`-style data-loss failure mode CLAUDE.md describes as
   already fixed elsewhere — it needs the same fix applied to these 2
   files before ingestion, or tuition-by-year queries will retrieve
   ambiguous, unattributable dollar figures.

3. **Fragmented sentences from inline HTML spans**: `faq.txt` (root) is
   the clearest example — bolded/linked phrases were extracted onto their
   own line, breaking sentences into 2-3 line fragments. I did not find
   this pattern anywhere else in a targeted read, but `faq.txt` alone (at
   20K chars, one of the largest files in the corpus) is a big enough
   piece of the corpus's total content to be worth a light fix.

4. No cookie banners, "Skip to content," or nav-boilerplate junk were
   found anywhere in the corpus (checked via grep across all folders) —
   the scraper's `<main>`/`<article>` extraction is clean on that front.
   The only recurring low-value boilerplate found is the "Learn More"
   link-text repeated ~15 times in `library/library-online-library-
   database.txt` — minor, self-limiting to one file.

5. **`handbook/` has no extracted text at all**, just a raw PDF — flagged
   above, and the biggest structural gap in the corpus relative to the
   task's assumptions.

## Recommendations

Based only on the numbers above, folders cluster into **four** groups, not
three — the data doesn't support a clean two-tier split.

### Group A — Short atomic pages, whole-file chunking (no splitting)
**leadership** (78.6% fit ≤1,500), **student-life** (72.7%), **scholarships**
(fits better at ≤2,000 — see note). Use `chunk_size=1500`,
`chunk_overlap=0` effectively meaning "don't split unless a file exceeds
the cap," with a `RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter` fallback (200 overlap)
only for the minority of files that overflow. Justification: leadership's
p90 is 1,718 — a 1,500 cap with a rare-overflow fallback keeps ~79% of
files as single, fully-coherent chunks; splitting an already-short profile
buys nothing and risks separating "Biography" from "Education."
Scholarships' median (1,640) sits just above 1,500 — bump the cap to
~1,800-2,000 for this group specifically so more scholarship pages stay
whole (criteria and required-documents belong together).

### Group B — Faculty: mostly whole-file, with a real split-needing tail
**faculty** doesn't fit cleanly in Group A despite the filename-based
assumption: only 60.4% fit in 1,500 chars (vs. leadership's 78.6%), and
p90 is 2,594 vs leadership's 1,718. Use the same `RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter`
config as Group A (1,500 cap, paragraph-first splitting) — it's not a
different *strategy*, just an acknowledgment that ~40% of files (the
long-bio ones like Yusuf Nulla) will legitimately produce 2 chunks per
profile. Because bios are already one-paragraph-per-line, a paragraph-
aware splitter cuts between paragraphs, not mid-sentence, for all but the
rare single 1,500+-char paragraph.

### Group C — Mixed prose, moderate length: split with paragraph-first splitter
**admissions, about, events, root-level files**. p90 ranges 4,763-10,560 —
these need real splitting, not whole-file. Recommend
`RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(chunk_size=1200, chunk_overlap=200,
separators=["\n\n", "\n", ". ", " "])`. Justification: blank-line
(`\n\n`) density is low everywhere in this corpus (1.6-3.9%), so relying
on paragraph breaks alone as the primary separator won't produce enough
cut points — falling back through single `\n` and sentence-ending
punctuation is necessary. 1,200/200 balances keeping a policy's stated
exception in the same chunk as its rule (admissions, e.g. the "as noted
below" list) against not producing enormous chunks for the longer pages
in this group (`faq.txt`, `admission-policies.txt`).

### Group D — Table-and-prose mixed, needs a custom row-aware splitter
**programs, academic-policies (partially), library (the 2 long catalog
files), tuition (after table fix)**. This is where a plain
`RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter` genuinely breaks: **20 files in
programs/ have table-row runs exceeding 800 chars**, and
`academic-calendar.txt` has runs that blow past 1,500. A char-count-based
splitter with `\n` as a separator will cut mid-table roughly as often as a
table run happens to straddle the `chunk_size` boundary — for programs/,
that's a near-certainty given 36.3% of all lines are table rows.

Concretely: treat a run of consecutive `^[^:]+: .+ \| [^:]+: .+` lines as
one atomic unit that the splitter is never allowed to break internally —
either (a) pack whole table rows into a chunk up to the size cap and start
a new chunk at a row boundary once the next row wouldn't fit, or (b) chunk
prose sections and table sections separately within the same file (prose
gets `chunk_size=1200/overlap=200` like Group C; the table block gets
packed by row up to the same cap) and tag both with the same source
metadata so retrieval doesn't care which sub-chunk it came from. Given
CLAUDE.md's emphasis on each table row already being self-contained
(headers repeated per row on purpose), packing whole rows is low-risk —
a chunk boundary between two course rows loses nothing, since neither row
depends on the other.

For `course-descriptions.txt` specifically (162KB, ~261 independent course
records): this file should not be chunked as prose at all. Split on the
course-code line (`^[A-Z]{2,5} [0-9]{2,3}[A-Z]? - `) so each chunk is
exactly one course's code + credits + description — that's both a more
natural retrieval unit ("what are the prerequisites for MATH 200?") and
avoids ever needing a mid-course char-count split, since individual course
records here run at most a few hundred chars.

For `academic-and-student-policies-and-regulations.txt` (55KB, ~20
sub-policies concatenated with bare title lines, no markdown headers):
treat each bare title line (`Academic Advising`, `Academic Honors`, ...)
as a manual section boundary the same way course codes are used above,
then apply the Group C prose splitter (1200/200) within each section.
This keeps a GPA-band rule and its immediate neighbor lines together far
more reliably than blind char-counting across the whole 55KB file.

### Where the data contradicts the prior filename-based assumptions

- **"faculty/ + leadership/ = short atomic profiles, don't split"** — true
  for leadership (78.6% fit in 1,500) but only partially true for faculty
  (60.4% fit in 1,500; p90 is 2,594, not clearly "short"). Faculty needs
  the same whole-file default *plus* a real splitting fallback for the
  ~40% long-bio tail — it's not safe to hardcode "never split" for this
  folder.
- **"academic-policies/ + handbook/ = dense prose with cross-references,
  larger chunks (1200) + bigger overlap (250)"** — academic-policies is
  NOT uniformly dense prose. Its median file (873 chars) is actually
  *shorter* than programs' or admissions', and 38.5% of its files fit
  whole in an 800-char chunk. The "dense prose" characterization only
  fits a handful of its files (`academic-and-student-policies-and-
  regulations.txt`, `graduation-application-and-requirements.txt`); the
  folder's most extreme file (`course-descriptions.txt`, 162KB) isn't
  prose at all, it's ~261 concatenated database-like records, and its
  second-most extreme (`academic-calendar.txt`) is 83% table rows. A
  single 1200/250 setting applied folder-wide would be wrong for most of
  academic-policies' files (over-sized for the short stubs, and
  structurally wrong — char-based, not row/record-aware — for the two
  huge outliers). And handbook/ has no text to chunk at all, so this
  assumption doesn't apply to it yet regardless of chunk size.
- **"everything else = the validated default (800/150)"** — programs/ in
  particular is a bad fit for 800: only 5.5% of its files fit whole in
  800 chars, and 20 of its 55 files have table-row runs that exceed 800
  chars on their own, meaning an 800-char cap with plain `\n` splitting
  will slice through a course table in over a third of program pages.
  Library's 2 long catalog pages and root's `faq.txt` have the same
  "800 is too small, mid-table/mid-list breaks likely" problem despite
  not being explicitly called out in the original assumptions.
